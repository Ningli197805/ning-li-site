Shanghai Marathon reflections

Shanghai Marathon, 30 November 2020, finish time 3 hours 56 minutes. This was my second marathon. Because it was still during the pandemic, the field was much smaller than planned. I obtained a place through a channel that cost about RMB 3,600, which felt expensive at the time. I remember clearly that at the start everyone had to stand about one metre apart, looking much like a formation at a sports meeting. We also had to wear masks and dispose of them in bins after about the first kilometre. There may have been more volunteers than runners. I may never see an event like that again. My parents flew from Shenzhen to Shanghai to cheer for me. We planned the route in advance so they could see me on the left side of the course at about 5 km and 13 km. I met them twice during the race, and having my family there was very exciting. At around halfway, I noticed a runner with dedicated support (I later learned the term “private pacer”). The pacer guided the runner, handed over water and reminded them of the pace. I saw that their pace was similar to mine, so I followed them for a long time, though I lost some time whenever I stopped for water. After around 30 km, that private pacer realised I had been following them and also fetched water for me. I was very grateful. I asked whether our pace could break four hours, and he said yes, perhaps even close to 3:50. I was thrilled. Later, at around 37 km, they sped up. I chose not to follow and instead held my own rhythm to finish the whole race. I set a personal best and broke four hours, achieving it in only my second full marathon. I was extremely happy, and after the race I had a seafood dinner with the friends from Shenzhen who had also raced.
